# Patterns — Techniques & Methods

## FINVIZ Descriptive Tab Filter
**When to use**: first pass of stock screening, before any fundamental analysis.
**How**: select one Sector; set Market Capitalization ≥$300M (prefer ≥$2B); set Dividend Yield ≥2%; optionally narrow by Industry. Ignore Analyst Recommendations, Average Volume, Float, and IPO Date — not useful for long-term investing.
**Trade-offs**: fast and simple, but only screens on "big picture" attributes — still requires the Fundamental tab pass to assess company health.

## FINVIZ Fundamental Tab Filter
**When to use**: second pass, applied within the sector already selected in the Descriptive tab.
**How**: set P/E <20, P/B <3, EPS growth (5yr) Positive, ROA >5%, ROE >10%, Debt/Equity ≤60–70%, Net Profit Margin Positive — then adjust every threshold against that sector's typical range (Ch5 reference table).
**Trade-offs**: mechanical and repeatable, but a universal threshold misreads sector-normal companies (e.g., REITs' high debt, Financials' misleadingly low P/E/P/B) — sector-relative judgment is required, not just the raw filter.

## Sector Recommendation Filter
**When to use**: before individual stock screening, to decide which 4-5 sectors to search at all.
**How**: favor sectors low in business-cycle sensitivity, with tangible book value, reliable (not legally-forced) dividends, and high barriers to competitive entry — Basic Materials, Consumer Defensive, Healthcare, Industrials, Utilities. Avoid Financial (most crisis-vulnerable), Technology (overpriced, low book value), and Consumer Cyclical (discretionary spending collapses first) unless deliberately accepting that risk.
**Trade-offs**: skipping Financial/Technology means missing two of the largest, most liquid sectors and forgoing their historical growth — a deliberate risk-reduction trade, not a claim those sectors can't perform well.

## Price Action Institutional-Activity Read
**When to use**: as the first lens applied to any stock chart, before Volume Profile.
**How**: identify Rotation (sideways — accumulation/distribution in progress), Trend (directional — reveals which way institutions were positioning), and Strong Rejection (sudden reversal — marks a future battlefield level).
**Trade-offs**: qualitative and requires practice to read fluently; Volume Profile (below) turns the same read into a precise, tradeable price level.

## Volume Accumulation Setup
**When to use**: a rotation/tight channel is visible, followed by a strong trend that has already started or completed.
**How**: (1) confirm rotation → strong trend structure; (2) apply Volume Profile inside the rotation to find the heaviest-volume zone; (3) place a limit order at that zone (prefer the start of the zone over the POC) and wait — possibly months or years — for price to pull back into it. Only trade zones that haven't been tested yet.
**Trade-offs**: the author's favorite setup, but requires significant patience; a false-breakout-prone alternative (buying the initial breakout) is explicitly rejected in favor of this pullback approach.

## Trend Setup
**When to use**: a strong, already-established uptrend is in progress and you want to buy a pullback within it rather than only at the rotation that started it.
**How**: (1) confirm a clear, strong uptrend (ideally Weekly chart); (2) apply Volume Profile within the trend to find a Volume Cluster (a "bump" of heavier volume where institutions paused); (3) place a limit order at the start of that cluster and wait for the pullback. Only trade untested clusters.
**Trade-offs**: works within trends already underway (doesn't require waiting for a full rotation-to-trend cycle), but clusters can be smaller/less obvious than a full rotation zone, requiring more chart-reading practice.

## Split-Entry Position Sizing
**When to use**: a stock has two (or three) identifiable Volume Profile Supports at different price levels.
**How**: allocate half the position's capital to the shallower Support, the other half to the deeper Support if price continues falling; optionally reserve a third tranche (from the portfolio's 20% reserve) for an extreme "catastrophic" Support far below current price.
**Trade-offs**: avoids needing to predict exactly how far a pullback will go, and rewards deeper discounts with proportionally more shares bought — but ties up capital across multiple trigger levels rather than deploying it all at once.

## Country/Index Selection Filter
**When to use**: before choosing which country's stock index to invest in (as an alternative or complement to individual stock picking).
**How**: check (1) interest rates — avoid economies dependent on sustained zero/negative-rate stimulus; (2) sovereign credit rating — require non-investment grade (speculative) or better from Moody's/S&P/Fitch; (3) sector composition via a heatmap tool — avoid indexes dominated by Financial/Technology if you want to avoid those sectors.
**Trade-offs**: filters out clearly risky economies, but ratings agencies are themselves imperfect (missed 2008), so this should combine with, not replace, independent judgment.

## Sell-Signal Combination
**When to use**: periodically reviewing existing holdings for exit timing.
**How**: watch for all three to align — (1) the relevant broad index at/near all-time highs; (2) the position already carries a large gain; (3) the company's P/E has drifted ~50%+ above its sector average. Scale out gradually as signals intensify, redeploying freed capital into corporate bond ETFs.
**Trade-offs**: deliberately conservative/gradual — won't catch an exact top, but avoids the whipsaw of reacting to any single noisy signal.

## No-Bright-Future Override
**When to use**: rare — when a company's or entire industry's fundamental prospects have permanently changed for the worse (regulatory shift, technological obsolescence, structural demand collapse).
**How**: sell regardless of current price, index level, or unrealized gain/loss — this overrides the standard sell-signal combination and the "don't change your time horizon" discipline.
**Trade-offs**: subjective and easy to misapply as an excuse for panic-selling during ordinary volatility — reserve strictly for genuine structural change, not short-term bad news.

## Money-Management Sizing (Portfolio-Level)
**When to use**: constructing the full portfolio from a fundamentally-qualified, Volume-Profile-confirmed watchlist (~20 stocks).
**How**: allocate a fixed 4% of capital per stock (never overweight favorites), split into two 2% tranches at two Supports; hold 20% of total capital in reserve for rare deep-discount opportunities; no leverage; no Stop Loss (Book Value is the natural floor); execute via limit orders.
**Trade-offs**: caps upside concentration in any single winner in exchange for structural downside protection and near-zero active-monitoring requirements.
