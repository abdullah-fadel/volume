## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The central method (two independent skill sets, always combined)**: (1) fundamental screening — use FINVIZ to filter stocks by sector, size, and financial health; (2) technical timing — use Price Action + Volume Profile to find the exact price to enter. Fundamentals answer "what to buy," Volume Profile answers "when." Never buy on one alone.

**Why invest at all**: cash loses real value to inflation every year (long-run global average ~5.4%/year since 1981) — stocks and indexes are structurally inflation-resistant because the same money-supply expansion that causes inflation also tends to lift stock prices.

**Index investing** (simplest path): buying an index buys a market-cap-weighted slice of every constituent — no stock-picking skill, no management fee, works with small capital, inflation-resistant. Big weakness: you can't opt out of a heavily-weighted sector you dislike (S&P 500 is often 40%+ Financial + Technology). Before choosing a country's index, check interest rates (avoid stimulus-dependent economies) and sovereign rating (non-investment grade or better) — and still wait for a price discount even on a fundamentally ideal index.

**Stock investing** (more control, more effort): lets you cherry-pick only the sectors/companies you want, at the cost of more fees and more diversification work. Always analyze a stock as if buying the entire company, not a ticker.

**Sector selection is the highest-leverage fundamental decision**: pick 4-5 sectors, favoring ones with low business-cycle sensitivity, real tangible book value, and reliable dividends. **Recommended**: Basic Materials, Consumer Defensive, Healthcare, Industrials, Utilities. **Not recommended**: Financial (most crisis-vulnerable, deceptively low P/E and P/B), Technology (overpriced, thin book value), Consumer Cyclical (discretionary spending collapses first). Always read every ratio *relative to its sector's normal range* — there is no universal cutoff.

**FINVIZ screening — 4 Descriptive-tab filters + 7 Fundamental-tab filters** (baseline values, sector-adjust every one):
- Descriptive: Market Cap ≥$300M (prefer ≥$2B), Dividend Yield ≥2%, Sector, Industry.
- Fundamental: P/E <20, P/B <3, EPS growth 5yr Positive, ROA >5%, ROE >10%, Debt/Equity ≤60-70%, Net Profit Margin Positive.
- After the automated filter: open each company's profile and manually read the numbers; investigate any anomalously good ratio (e.g., P/E <5) via recent news before trusting it.

**Price Action — three institutional-activity signatures** (read before applying Volume Profile): **Rotation** (sideways — institutions quietly building/exiting a position), **Trend** (directional move revealing which way they were positioning), **Strong Rejection** (sudden reversal marking a future battlefield level). Price moves from aggression (willingness to transact at a worse price now), never from "more buyers than sellers" — every trade needs a counterparty.

**Volume Profile — the timing tool**: a Price+Time+Volume histogram; wide sections = heavy institutional trading = Support. Shapes: **D** (balance, building positions), **P** (post-uptrend rotation), **b** (post-downtrend rotation), **Thin** (fast trend, small Volume Clusters only).

**The two entry setups** (never chase a stock at new highs — always wait for the pullback):
1. **Volume Accumulation Setup**: find a rotation followed by a strong trend → apply Volume Profile *inside the rotation* → mark the heaviest-volume zone → wait (limit order) for price to pull back into it. Untested zones only.
2. **Trend Setup**: find an established strong trend → apply Volume Profile *within the trend* → mark the Volume Cluster (a "bump") → wait for the pullback into it. Untested clusters only.
- Enter at the *start* of the heavy-volume zone (fewer missed fills) rather than waiting specifically for the Point of Control (POC).
- Split a position across two Supports (half at each) when two viable levels exist.
- Expect roughly a 65-70% hit rate — a bad entry on a fundamentally strong stock is a minor setback, not a disaster.

**When to sell**: don't act on any single signal — wait for **all** of (a) the broad index near all-time highs, (b) a large realized/unrealized gain, and (c) company P/E ~50%+ above its sector average, then scale out gradually (park freed capital in bond ETFs). Separately, sell regardless of price if a company/industry's future has permanently, structurally changed (Buffett's "no bright future" rule — e.g., newspapers vs. internet media).

**Building the portfolio**: target ~20 fundamentally-qualified, Volume-Profile-confirmed stocks, each a fixed 4% of capital (never overweight favorites), split into two 2% tranches at two Supports. Hold a 20% cash reserve for a rare, much deeper 3rd Support. No leverage. No Stop Loss (Book Value is the natural floor). Execute via limit orders, not active monitoring. Park idle capital in corporate bond ETFs (not cash or dividend stocks — those fall in the same sell-offs that create your buying opportunities). Own actual shares, not CFDs (daily swap fee compounds against multi-year holds); use fractional shares for expensive stocks in small accounts.

**Discipline over analysis**: most investor mistakes are failures of discipline after a sound plan, not failures of analysis — don't extrapolate past price behavior, don't chase all-time highs, don't switch to faster timeframes, don't second-guess a limit order at the last minute, and be willing to buy during market-wide panics (structurally when this method's discounts appear).
