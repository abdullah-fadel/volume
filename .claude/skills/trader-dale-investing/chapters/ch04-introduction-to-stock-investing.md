# Chapter 4: Introduction to Stock Investing

## Core Idea
Individual stock investing trades index investing's simplicity for control — you can cherry-pick only the companies and sectors you want — but it costs more time, more transaction fees, and requires more diversification effort; the author's whole method exists to make that extra effort systematic rather than guesswork.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Stocks vs. bonds (why companies issue stock)**: a company needing capital can issue debt (bonds — must be repaid with interest) or issue stock (sells a permanent ownership slice, never repaid, but dilutes the founders' share forever). Understanding this distinction clarifies what you actually own when you buy a stock: a real, permanent slice of the business, not a lottery ticket.
- **Reject economic/valuation models, use a screener instead**: rather than academic models (Beta-based risk, CAPM-style reasoning), the author's method is (1) use a stock screener to filter out unhealthy/risky companies by sector and fundamentals, then (2) use Volume Profile to time the entry. This two-step method is the structural backbone of the rest of the book.

## Key Concepts
- **Cherry-picking**: stock investing's core advantage over index investing — you select only companies/sectors you find genuinely investable.
- **Diversification cost**: with stocks (unlike an index), diversification requires actively buying many separate positions, each with its own transaction fee and analysis time.
- **"Our Edge"**: not deeper analysis than internet stock forums, but combining ordinary fundamental filtering with disciplined, patient timing via Volume Profile — a tool most retail stock investors never use.

## Mental Models
- Treat a stock purchase exactly like buying a private business outright — analyze the whole company, not just the ticker.
- Treat stock-picking as "shooting 20 bullets and hitting the target 15 times" rather than "shooting 1000 bullets blindfolded" (the penny-stock approach) — precision over volume.

## Anti-patterns
- **Chasing "hidden gem" penny/small-cap stocks**: statistically only ~1 in 100 succeed; the strategy only works if you buy hundreds of them to catch the rare winner, which contradicts the low-effort, concentrated-conviction approach this book teaches.
- **Making rocket science out of stock analysis**: excessive analytical complexity (deep economic modeling) doesn't reliably outperform a disciplined, simple filtering process — the author's own former colleagues at a brokerage, some of the sharpest people he knew, didn't rely on academic models either.

## Key Takeaways
1. Stock investing gives you cherry-picking control that index investing doesn't, at the cost of more time and more transaction fees.
2. View every stock purchase as if you were buying the whole company, not a ticker.
3. Avoid penny stocks / pink sheets as a core strategy — the odds require scale (hundreds of small bets) that conflicts with a concentrated, patient approach.
4. The rest of the book's method is two-step: fundamental screening (Ch5–Ch7) to find *what* to buy, then Price Action + Volume Profile (Ch8–Ch10) to find *when* to buy it.
