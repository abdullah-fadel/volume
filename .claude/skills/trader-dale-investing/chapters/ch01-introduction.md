# Chapter 1: Introduction — Why Invest, and the Author's Approach

## Core Idea
Inflation permanently erodes cash savings, so the only defense is putting money to work; the author's method — building a long-term stock/index portfolio using fundamentals to pick companies and Price Action + Volume Profile to time entries — is deliberately built for people who do **not** want to become full-time traders.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Buy-the-whole-company mindset**: when evaluating any stock, analyze it as if you were buying 100% of the company, not just a ticker symbol — because your % return is identical whether you own 0.0000001% or all of it. This reframes every stock decision as a business decision, not a speculation.
  - When to use: before every stock purchase, as a sanity check against buying "a number going up."
  - How: read the company like an owner would — does the business itself (not just the chart) look investable?
- **Low-effort, low-attention investing**: the strategy is explicitly designed to require infrequent monitoring (a watchlist of pending limit orders, checked roughly weekly) rather than active, chart-watching trading.

## Key Concepts
- **Ceteris paribus critique**: academic economic/valuation models hold "everything else equal," which never holds in real markets (pandemics, tweets, unrelated scandals) — the author distrusts complex models for this reason.
- **Beta as a risk measure — rejected**: standard portfolio theory treats volatility (Beta) as risk; the author argues a fundamentally sound stock getting cheaper (e.g., Coca-Cola dropping in a market-wide sell-off) is a *better* opportunity, not a riskier one.
- **The "edge"**: not out-analyzing the internet's most obsessive stock pickers, but combining ordinary fundamental filtering with a professional-grade timing tool (Volume Profile) that most retail investors never use.

## Mental Models
- Think of academic valuation models as weather forecasts for yesterday — technically correct about the past, useless for the future.
- Treat your portfolio like a slow-cooked meal, not a stove you keep checking — low monitoring frequency is a feature of this method, not a shortcut.

## Anti-patterns
- **Relying on brokerage "manager" sales advice**: the author's own experience as an analyst showed sales staff built persuasive arguments for products regardless of actual merit, and often didn't invest their own money.
- **Overcomplicating stock analysis with academic models**: adds false precision without improving real-world decisions.

## Key Takeaways
1. The goal is smart, low-effort long-term investing — not day trading or full-time market obsession.
2. Evaluate every stock as if you were buying the entire company.
3. Reject risk models built purely on price volatility (Beta); a temporary price drop in a fundamentally sound company is a discount, not new risk.
4. The rest of this skill builds two independent skill sets that combine: (a) fundamental stock/sector selection via a screener, and (b) Price Action + Volume Profile for timing entries.
