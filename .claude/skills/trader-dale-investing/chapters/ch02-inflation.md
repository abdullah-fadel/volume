# Chapter 2: Inflation — The Reason You Must Invest

## Core Idea
Cash sitting in a bank account is not "safe" — it is a guaranteed, silent loss of purchasing power to inflation, which is why the entire book's premise is to put money to work rather than save it passively.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Inflation as an invisible tax**: inflation transfers real wealth away from cash-holders without any visible transaction — the nominal balance stays the same while real buying power shrinks.
  - When to use: whenever evaluating whether "just saving" is an acceptable strategy — it isn't, beyond an emergency-fund horizon.
  - How: compute real value = nominal amount ÷ (1 + inflation rate)^years. At a long-run global average of ~5.4% (since 1981), $10,000 shrinks to roughly $7,763 in real terms after 10 years at 2.5% inflation, or to $6,648 after 10 years at 4% inflation.
- **Two-tier inflation response**: (1) minimum viable response — buy Inflation-Linked Bonds (ILBs) / inflation-protected bond ETFs just to keep pace with inflation; (2) full response — build a stock/index portfolio to actually grow real wealth, not just tread water.

## Key Concepts
- **Inflation-Linked Bonds (ILBs)**: government-issued bonds whose payouts are indexed to inflation, sold as ETFs (e.g., SPDR Bloomberg 1-10 Year TIPS ETF, Schwab U.S. TIPS ETF, Vanguard VTIP) — the simplest possible inflation hedge, appropriate if beating inflation is your *only* goal.
- **Money supply → stock prices link**: central banks expanding the money supply (low/zero rates, stimulus) is the same force that inflates consumer prices *and* pushes stock prices up — stocks are naturally inflation-correlated because company revenues and asset prices rise together with the money supply.

## Mental Models
- Think of stocks and stock indexes as inflation *shelters*, not inflation *victims* — unlike cash, equity ownership tends to move with the same monetary forces that cause inflation in the first place.
- Use ILBs as the "do nothing else" baseline; anyone willing to learn stock/index investing should aim higher than merely covering inflation.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating a stable bank balance as safe**: the number staying flat while real purchasing power falls is the core trap the whole book is written to prevent.
- **Assuming central banks can raise rates freely to fight inflation**: the author notes economies have become "addicted" to cheap money, so rate hikes are politically/economically constrained — don't expect inflation to self-correct quickly via policy.

## Key Takeaways
1. Money in a bank account loses real value every year inflation exceeds the interest paid — this is the entire justification for the book's method.
2. If your only goal is to *match* inflation, ILB/TIPS ETFs are sufficient and low-effort.
3. Stocks and stock indexes are structurally advantaged over cash and many other asset classes during inflationary periods because they're driven by the same money-supply expansion that causes inflation.
4. The book's ambition is higher than beating inflation: build a portfolio that grows real wealth.
