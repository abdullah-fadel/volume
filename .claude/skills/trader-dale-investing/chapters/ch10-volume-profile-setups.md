# Chapter 10: Volume Profile Trading Setups

## Core Idea
Two setups — the Volume Accumulation Setup (heavy volume inside a pre-trend rotation) and the Trend Setup (a Volume Cluster inside an ongoing trend) — are how the author converts "this stock looks fundamentally good" into "buy it at this exact price," by waiting for the price to pull back into a level where institutions already committed heavy volume, never chasing a stock at new highs.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Volume Profile Setup #1 — Volume Accumulation Setup**: the author's favorite setup.
  1. Find a price rotation/tight channel followed by a strong uptrend — the rotation is where institutions built their position before the trend.
  2. Apply Volume Profile *inside the rotation* to find where the heaviest volume traded — that zone is the Support.
  3. Wait for price to pull back into that heavy-volume zone (it may take months or years) and buy there — using a limit order or alert, not active monitoring.
  - **Validity condition**: the zone must not have been tested yet (no prior pullback into it) — first tests have better odds than already-tested zones.
  - **Why not trade the breakout instead**: breakouts from the rotation are prone to false signals (price often re-enters the rotation) — waiting for the pullback into the zone is more reliable.
- **Volume Profile Setup #2 — Trend Setup**: for a strong, already-established uptrend.
  1. Confirm a clear, strong uptrend, ideally on a Weekly chart.
  2. Apply Volume Profile within the trend to find a Volume Cluster — a localized "bump" where institutions paused to add to positions.
  3. Wait for a pullback into that cluster (limit order) and buy there.
  - **Validity condition**: same as Setup #1 — the cluster must not have already been tested.
- **Entry point choice (both setups)**: two valid options — (a) the *beginning* of the heavy-volume zone (author's preference — reduces the chance of missing the fill if price turns before reaching the deepest point), or (b) the **Point of Control (POC)**, the single price with the heaviest volume within the zone (theoretically the "best" price, but the trade is missed more often if price reverses early).
- **Two reasons the levels hold** (applies to both setups): (1) institutions that built a position at the level are motivated to defend it by buying more when price returns; (2) opposing traders caught on the wrong side near the level don't want to fight strong institutional buyers, so they exit — and exiting a short position means buying, reinforcing the same upward push.
- **Splitting entries across two Supports**: when a stock has two viable heavy-volume zones, enter half the position at the first (shallower) Support and the other half at the second (deeper) Support if price continues falling — captures a bigger discount without requiring perfect timing.

## Key Concepts
- **Point of Control (POC)**: the single price level with the heaviest traded volume within a given Volume Profile zone.
- **Untested zone**: a Support that price has not yet returned to since it was formed — the author's stated preference, since a level's first retest statistically reacts better than a level that's already been tested once.

## Mental Models
- Treat "wait for the price to come back to the zone" as the entire timing philosophy — never chase, always wait, use limit orders so you don't need to actively watch the chart.
- Expect roughly 65–70% of setups to work out — this is a game of probability, not certainty; a bad entry on a fundamentally good stock is a minor setback, not a disaster, because good stocks tend to recover.

## Anti-patterns
- **Trading the breakout from a rotation instead of waiting for the pullback**: prone to false breakouts (price re-enters the range) — not the author's method.
- **Requiring a confirmed reaction before entering**: both setups enter on the *first* touch of the qualifying zone via a limit order — no extra confirmation candle is required.
- **Re-entering an already-tested zone**: once a Support has been retested once, it's no longer considered a fresh, high-probability setup.
- **Panic-selling stocks during a market-wide crash (e.g., COVID-19) if fundamentals haven't changed**: the author explicitly recommends holding through such drops (price can overshoot Supports) if the underlying company is still fundamentally sound, and even views a market-wide crash as an opportunity to buy other discounted stocks.

## Worked Example
**AbbVie (ABBV), Daily/Weekly chart** — used by the author as the primary illustration of Setup #1. The chart shows a long rotation where massive volume traded (institutional accumulation), followed by a sharp uptrend to new highs. Rather than chasing the new highs, the correct action is to mark the heavy-volume zone from the rotation and wait — patiently, via a limit order — for price to pull back into that zone, however long it takes (the author notes this can take up to a year or more). When price eventually returns to the marked zone, that is the entry.

A second pattern, **Bank of America (BAC), Weekly chart**, illustrates a risk case: price reacted to the *beginning* of the heavy-volume zone rather than reaching the POC (missing the "better" price if you'd waited for POC specifically — reinforcing the author's preference for entering at the start of the zone). It also shows a sharp COVID-19-driven sell-off through the level, which recovered only because of extraordinary Federal Reserve intervention — a reminder that the Financial sector (Ch5) carries structural crisis risk even when a specific setup looks technically clean.

A **Home Depot (HD), Weekly chart** example shows the "split entry" technique: two Volume Clusters formed within the same uptrend; the recommended approach is to buy half the position at the shallower cluster, and if price drops further to the second, deeper cluster, buy the remaining half there — capturing a bigger discount if the deeper pullback occurs, without needing to predict which will happen.

## Key Takeaways
1. Never buy a stock at its historical high — always locate a heavy-volume zone (via Setup #1 or #2) and wait for price to discount back into it.
2. Setup #1 (Volume Accumulation) uses the rotation *before* a trend; Setup #2 (Trend Setup) uses a Volume Cluster *within* an ongoing trend.
3. Enter at the start of the heavy-volume zone (safer fill) rather than waiting specifically for the POC (better price, but easier to miss).
4. Use limit orders, not active monitoring — patience is structural to this method, not optional discipline.
5. Expect a ~65–70% hit rate; a bad entry on a fundamentally strong stock (Ch5–Ch7) is a minor issue, not a disaster.
6. When two viable Supports exist, split the position across both rather than betting everything on one precise level.
