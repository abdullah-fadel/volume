# Cheatsheet — Trader Dale's Investing Decision Rules

## Core Filter (never skip either half)
Every buy needs: (1) the stock passes fundamental screening (sector-adjusted P/E, P/B, EPS growth, ROA, ROE, Debt/Equity, Net Margin) AND (2) price has pulled back into a Volume-Profile-confirmed Support (Accumulation Setup or Trend Setup). Fundamentals alone tell you *what*; Volume Profile alone tells you *where* — you need both to know *what and when*.

## Screening Thresholds (baseline — always sector-adjust, see Ch5 table)
| Filter | Baseline | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Market Cap | ≥ $300M, prefer ≥ $2B | Bigger = more time-tested |
| Dividend Yield | ≥ 2% | 2-4% healthy; double-digit = investigate sustainability |
| P/E | < 20 | Loosen to 25 for naturally high-P/E sectors; <5 = red flag, not bargain |
| P/B | < 3 | 2-2.5 for Energy/Industrials/Utilities; 3.5 for Tech/Healthcare; use P/NAV for REITs |
| EPS growth (5yr) | Positive | Prefer 5yr over 1yr figures |
| ROA | > 5% (10%+ for narrower search) | Compare within sector only |
| ROE | > 10% (15-20%+ for narrower search) | Always higher than ROA for same company |
| Debt/Equity | ≤ 60-70%, avoid <10-20% and >100% | Loosen heavily for Utilities/Energy/Financials/REITs (REIT norm ~350%) |
| Net Profit Margin | Positive only | Low-priority filter; wide sector variance |

## Sector Quick-Read
| Recommended | Not Recommended | Situational |
|---|---|---|
| Basic Materials, Consumer Defensive, Healthcare, Industrials, Utilities | Financial (most crisis-vulnerable), Technology (overpriced/low book value), Consumer Cyclical (discretionary) | Communication Services (mega-cap dominated), Energy (political risk), Real Estate/REITs (rate-sensitive, dividend-only case) |

Pick 4-5 sectors total. Fewer = under-diversified; more = might as well buy the index (cheaper).

## Country/Index Selection
| Check | Rule |
|---|---|
| Interest rates | Avoid economies dependent on sustained zero/negative-rate stimulus |
| Sovereign rating | Require non-investment grade (speculative) or better, Moody's/S&P/Fitch |
| Sector heatmap | Avoid indexes >40% concentrated in Financial+Technology if avoiding those sectors |
| Price level | Even a fundamentally ideal index/stock: wait for a discount, don't buy at highs |

## Volume Profile Setup Selection
| Chart shows... | Use | Entry |
|---|---|---|
| Rotation followed by a strong trend (already happened or forming) | Volume Accumulation Setup | Heaviest-volume zone *inside* the rotation |
| Strong uptrend already established, with a pause/bump | Trend Setup | Volume Cluster *inside* the trend |
| Two viable Support zones on the same stock | Split entry | Half at shallower, half at deeper Support |

**Entry point rule**: prefer the *start* of the heavy-volume zone over the exact Point of Control (POC) — fewer missed fills, small cost in "best possible price."
**Validity rule**: only trade *untested* zones — once retested, no longer a fresh setup.
**Never**: chase a breakout from the rotation (false-breakout prone); buy at a new all-time high without a pullback.

## Portfolio Construction
| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Number of positions | ~20 stocks (funnel: ~65 fundamentally qualified → ~40 after sector cuts → ~20 after Volume Profile confirms a Support) |
| Per-stock allocation | 4% of capital, fixed and equal (never overweight favorites) |
| Entry structure | 2% at Support #1, 2% at Support #2 |
| Cash reserve | 20%, held for a rare, much deeper 3rd Support |
| Leverage | None |
| Stop Loss | None (Book Value = natural floor) |
| Order type | Limit orders (or alerts), not active monitoring |
| Idle capital | Corporate bond ETFs (low/negative stock correlation), not cash or dividend stocks |
| Vehicle | Actual shares, not CFDs (daily swap fee compounds against long holds) |
| Small accounts | Fractional shares to keep expensive stocks within the 4% cap |

## Sell Signal Combination (need multiple, not just one)
| Signal | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Broad index level | At/near all-time highs → hold only ~50% in stocks, scale toward 10-20% as it climbs further |
| Position gain | Already large/realized gain present |
| Sector-relative P/E | Company P/E ≥ ~50% above its sector average |
| Override (any price/gain level) | "No bright future" — company/industry structurally, permanently impaired (Buffett rule) |

## Red Flags / Tells
- Ultra-low P/E (<5) → market already pricing in bad news; check recent news before buying, don't assume bargain.
- Misleadingly low P/E and P/B together (esp. Financial sector) → often reflects leverage risk, not value.
- Debt/Equity that looks "too clean" (near 0) → possible under-investment in growth, not automatically safer.
- Checking your portfolio/charts more than ~weekly → sign you're about to make an emotional, plan-breaking decision.
- Wanting to cancel or front-run a limit order at the last minute → classic last-minute-change mistake; let the plan execute.
- Financial media framing a sell-off as pure danger → normal; this strategy requires buying into exactly this sentiment.

## Discipline Rules (from Common Mistakes, Ch13)
Don't extrapolate past charts forward. Don't buy at all-time highs. Don't chase penny stocks. Don't concentrate in one stock or one sector. Don't switch to faster timeframes once you've planned on Daily/Weekly. Don't abandon your multi-year time horizon early (absent a "no bright future" override). Don't buy-before/sell-after a dividend for "capture" — it's a wash after the ex-dividend price drop and taxes. Don't invest money needed within the next year or so. Do be willing to buy during panics — that's structurally when the discounts appear.
