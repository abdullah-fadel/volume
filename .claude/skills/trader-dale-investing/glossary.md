# Glossary — Stock Investing with Volume Profile (Trader Dale)

**Aggression (drives price)** — the willingness of buyers or sellers to transact at a worse price to get filled now; the actual mechanism that moves price, not the number of buyers vs. sellers (Ch8)

**b-Shaped Profile** — Volume Profile shape mirroring P-shaped: aggressive sellers pushed price down, then heavy-volume rotation followed; typical in a downtrend or at the end of an uptrend (Ch9)

**Big Three (rating agencies)** — Moody's, S&P, and Fitch; the main sovereign/corporate credit rating agencies, imperfect but useful as a screening filter (Ch3)

**Blue chips** — the large, stable companies that dominate major stock indexes (Ch3)

**Book Value** — Total Assets minus Total Liabilities; represents the worst-case per-share recovery if a company liquidated; functions as a natural "Stop Loss" floor in this strategy (Ch6, Ch12)

**Ceteris paribus** — "all else equal"; the assumption underlying most academic valuation models, which the author argues never holds in real markets (Ch1)

**D-Shaped Profile** — the most common Volume Profile shape: temporary balance, institutions building positions ahead of the next big move (Ch9)

**Debt/Equity** — Total Debt ÷ Total Equity; measures company leverage; recommended ≤60–70% (tighter/looser depending on sector norms) (Ch6)

**Dividend Yield** — latest annual dividend ÷ current stock price; recommended screening filter ≥2% (Ch5)

**EPS (Earnings Per Share)** — (Net Income − Dividends) ÷ Shares Outstanding; recommend screening on the 5-year growth figure, filtered to Positive (Ch6)

**Ex-dividend date** — the date after which a new buyer no longer receives the upcoming dividend; the share price mechanically drops by roughly the dividend amount on this date (Ch13)

**FINVIZ** — the stock screener used throughout the book (Descriptive tab + Fundamental tab); limited to companies quoted on AMEX/NASDAQ/NYSE (Ch4, Ch5, Ch6, Ch7)

**Fractional Shares** — buying a dollar amount of a stock rather than a whole share, used to keep expensive stocks within a small account's position-sizing rules (Ch12)

**Free Capital Allocation** — deploying capital not yet used at a Support into corporate bond ETFs (rather than cash or dividend stocks) while waiting for entries (Ch12)

**Index (stock)** — a market-cap-weighted basket of the biggest companies in a chosen economy (e.g., S&P 500); buying it buys a proportional slice of every constituent (Ch3)

**Inflation-Linked Bonds (ILBs)** — government bonds whose payouts are indexed to inflation; the minimal-effort inflation hedge (Ch2)

**Limit Order** — a pending order that executes automatically once price reaches a specified level; the primary execution method recommended throughout, enabling low-monitoring investing (Ch10, Ch12)

**Market Capitalization** — total market value of a company's outstanding shares; recommended screening filter ≥$300M (prefer ≥$2B) (Ch5)

**mREIT (Mortgage REIT)** — a REIT that lends against mortgage-backed securities rather than owning property directly; higher, riskier dividends than standard REITs (Ch5)

**Net Profit Margin** — Net Profit ÷ Total Revenue; recommended screening filter Positive only, a low-priority filter due to wide sector variance (Ch6)

**"No bright future" rule** — Buffett-derived sell trigger: sell when a company's or industry's fundamental prospects have permanently, structurally deteriorated, regardless of price (Ch11)

**P-Shaped Profile** — Volume Profile shape signaling an uptrend: aggressive buyers pushed price up, then heavy-volume rotation followed (Ch9)

**P/B (Price to Book Ratio)** — Current Stock Price ÷ Book Value; recommended screening filter <3 (sector-adjusted) (Ch6)

**P/E (Price to Earnings Ratio)** — Current Stock Price ÷ EPS; recommended screening filter <20 (sector-adjusted); represents years to "earn back" the purchase price (Ch6)

**P/NAV** — Price to Net Asset Value; used in place of P/B for REITs, since book value poorly reflects real property value (Ch5)

**Payout Ratio** — the share of a company's earnings distributed as dividends vs. retained for growth (Ch5)

**Point of Control (POC)** — the single price level with the heaviest traded volume within a Volume Profile zone (Ch10)

**Rating (sovereign)** — a country's credit rating from the Big Three agencies; used as one filter (non-investment grade or better) when choosing which country's index to invest in (Ch3)

**REIT (Real Estate Investment Trust)** — a company holding a portfolio of real estate that must distribute ≥90% of income as dividends to avoid corporate tax, producing high yields (Ch5)

**ROA (Return on Assets)** — Net Income ÷ Total Assets; efficiency measure; recommended screening filter >5% (tighter for a narrower search) (Ch6)

**ROE (Return on Equity)** — Annual Earnings ÷ Equity; efficiency measure excluding debt effects; recommended screening filter >10% (Ch6)

**Rotation** — sideways price action; the Price Action signature of institutions quietly building or exiting a large position (Ch8)

**Sector (FINVIZ)** — one of 11 categories (Basic Materials, Communication Services, Consumer Cyclical, Consumer Defensive, Energy, Financial, Healthcare, Industrials, Real Estate, Technology, Utilities); only one selectable per screening pass (Ch5)

**Standard Portfolio Theory (critique)** — the academic framework using Beta to measure risk, rejected by the author as misclassifying a discounted, fundamentally-sound stock as "riskier" simply because it's more volatile (Ch12)

**Strong Rejection** — a sudden, aggressive price reversal marking a significant future battlefield level between buyers and sellers (Ch8)

**Support (Volume Profile)** — a heavy-volume price zone where price is likely to stop falling and reverse, because institutions who built positions there are motivated to defend the level (Ch9, Ch10)

**Swap (CFD overnight fee)** — a daily holding fee charged on CFD positions (even without leverage); the reason the author recommends actual stock ownership over stock CFDs for long-term holding (Ch12)

**Trend** — a directional price move caused by one side's aggression; typically follows a rotation once institutions finish building a position (Ch8)

**Trend Setup** — Volume Profile Setup #2: entering at a Volume Cluster found within an ongoing strong trend (Ch10)

**Value-weighted index** — an index where each constituent's influence is proportional to its market capitalization (e.g., Apple's ~6-7% weight in the S&P 500) (Ch3)

**Volume Accumulation Setup** — Volume Profile Setup #1: entering at the heaviest-volume zone found inside a rotation that preceded a strong trend (Ch10)

**Volume Cluster** — a localized "bump" of heavier volume inside an otherwise thin trend profile, marking a brief institutional pause mid-trend (Ch9, Ch10)

**Volume Profile** — a trading tool/indicator showing volume traded at each price level (a Price + Time + Volume histogram), used to locate institutional Support zones (Ch9)

**20% Reserve** — the portion of portfolio capital deliberately held back, undeployed, for a rare 3rd/deepest Support during extreme market discounts (Ch12)
