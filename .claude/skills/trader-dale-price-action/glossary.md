# Glossary

**AB=CD** — a four-point wave pattern (swings A, B, C, D) where the C-to-D leg is measured to equal the A-to-B leg via Fibonacci; D is the entry point (Ch6).

**Aggression** — the driving force behind all price movement: one side (buyers or sellers) using market orders to get filled immediately, absorbing liquidity and pushing price (Ch2).

**Aggressive initiation activity** — a sharp, one-sided trending move, often following sideways accumulation, that reveals the direction institutions committed to (Ch3).

**ATR (Average True Range)** — volatility indicator used to size Stop-loss/Profit Target values relative to an instrument's average daily range (Ch14, Ch18).

**b-profile** — Volume Profile shape resembling a lowercase "b"; forms with aggressive sellers and weak buyers, typically in a downtrend or possible uptrend exhaustion (Ch12).

**Bad losing trade** — a losing trade from poor/no analysis or broken discipline; treated as inexpensive tuition, not something to repeat (Ch20).

**Bad winning trade** — a winning trade from poor analysis or broken rules; considered the most dangerous trade type because it reinforces bad process (Ch20).

**Break-even (BE)** — Stop-loss moved to the entry price, eliminating downside risk on a trade (Ch18).

**Catastrophic scenario SL** — a hard backstop set at 150% of the "normal SL," used alongside the Alternative SL approach to cap worst-case risk (Ch18).

**Correlation** — the degree to which two instruments move alike, from -100% (opposite) to +100% (identical); high correlation across open positions creates excessive risk exposure (Ch15, Ch19).

**Cycle of Doom and Despair** — the failure loop of abandoning a strategy before mastering it and repeatedly jumping to new ones, never proving any single approach (Ch20).

**D-profile** — Volume Profile shape resembling a "D"; forms when the market is balanced (rotation/sideways), a sign of accumulation (Ch12).

**Daily Open** — the price at the New York close/new-day boundary (5:00 p.m. NY time); the author's strongest "session open" type support/resistance reference (Ch8).

**Excessive risk exposure** — holding multiple positions in heavily correlated instruments that can all move (and lose) together from one catalyst (Ch15, Ch19).

**Failed auction** — a swing high/low where more than one participant was still active at the extreme (no single "last" trade); an unresolved market imperfection that acts like a magnet, pulling price back to test through it (Ch11).

**Fixed Profit Target / Stop-loss** — a single ATR-derived pip value applied uniformly to every trade, for simplicity and consistency (Ch18).

**Flexible Volume Profile** — the author's tool for drawing a Volume Profile over any user-selected chart region rather than a fixed daily/weekly window (Ch12).

**Good losing trade ("hope-killer")** — a properly analyzed, flawlessly executed trade that still loses; a statistically normal outcome, not evidence the strategy is broken (Ch20).

**Good winning trade** — a properly analyzed, flawlessly executed trade that wins; the only trade type worth feeling proud of (Ch20).

**High water mark** — the highest account balance reached to date; the author's rebasing point for recalculating fixed-dollar risk per trade (Ch19).

**Institutional dominance** — the observation that roughly 80-90%+ of forex volume is transacted by a small number of large financial institutions, motivating the book's entire trade-with-smart-money framework (Ch1, Ch2, Ch24).

**Martingale** — a bet-doubling-after-loss money management system; mathematically guaranteed to eventually blow up an account regardless of edge (Ch22).

**Monster red news** — the author's top severity tier of macro news (e.g., Rate Decision, FOMC Meeting); capable of starting daily/weekly/monthly trends, avoided entirely for hours around release (Ch16).

**Neutral SL management** — moving Stop-loss to the reaction point once price reaches 70-80% of the Profit Target; the author's preferred trailing style for actively-watched sessions (Ch18).

**Open-drive** — a sudden, strong, one-sided price move, often following sideways accumulation or a session open; its starting point becomes a strong support/resistance level (Ch5).

**P-profile** — Volume Profile shape resembling a "P"; forms with aggressive buyers and weak sellers, typically in an uptrend or possible downtrend exhaustion (Ch12).

**Point of Control (POC)** — the single price level within a Volume Profile where the most volume traded; a strong reaction/target level, never traded without Price Action confirmation (Ch12).

**Price Action** — reading the naked chart's continuous price flow/dynamics (not isolated candlestick patterns) to infer institutional aggression and intent (Ch2).

**Reversal trade** — entering opposite your original direction at the same level after price shoots through it without reacting at all, applying the support-becomes-resistance logic to a failed setup (Ch13).

**RRR (Risk-Reward Ratio)** — the ratio of potential loss to potential gain on a trade; more positive RRR mechanically lowers strike rate for the same underlying edge (Ch19).

**Session Open** — the opening price of the Asian, European, or US trading session; a support/resistance orientation point used as confirmation only (Ch7).

**Sideways price action area** — a range where institutions slowly and discreetly accumulate a large position without moving price or alerting other participants (Ch3).

**Strong high/low** — a swing point formed by a fast, aggressive change of direction, indicating real support/resistance (Ch10).

**Strong rejection** — a sudden, aggressive price reversal where one side of the market overwhelms the other; marks a new support/resistance zone (Ch3).

**Support becoming resistance (and vice versa)** — a breached strong support/resistance level flips role rather than losing significance, and is traded on the retest (Ch4).

**Swap/rollover fee** — the interest-rate differential charged or paid for holding a forex position overnight; relevant for long-term positions (Ch14).

**Tested level** — a level judged "used up" because price already produced a strong reaction near it; discarded based on reaction size, zone width, sharpness, and trend context (Ch23).

**Thin profile** — Volume Profile shape formed during a strong, fast trend; mostly thin with small volume clusters marking where trend participants briefly added to positions (Ch12).

**Volume Accumulation Setup (Setup #1)** — entering at the heaviest-volume price within a sideways rotation area that preceded a strong directional move (Ch13).

**Volume cluster** — a localized concentration of heavy volume within a larger structure (e.g., a trend or rejection), marking where aggressive participants added to positions (Ch12, Ch13).

**Volume-based Profit Target / Stop-loss** — PT/SL placed relative to the nearest significant Volume Profile zone rather than a fixed pip count (Ch18).

**Volume Profile** — a histogram of traded volume at each price level (not at time), revealing where institutions concentrated activity (Ch12).

**Weak high/low** — a swing point formed slowly with no decisive aggressive rejection candle; likely to be tested through rather than respected (Ch10).

**Weak red news** — the author's lowest severity tier of macro news (e.g., Crude Oil Inventories, Unemployment Claims); safe to hold trades through (Ch16).
