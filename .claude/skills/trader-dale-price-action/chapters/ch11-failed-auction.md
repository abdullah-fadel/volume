# Chapter 11: Failed Auction

## Core Idea
Price movement is a continuous auction, and any swing high/low where more than one participant was still willing to trade at the extreme (no single "last guy" winner) is a "failed auction" — a market imperfection that acts like a magnet, drawing price back to test through it and complete the auction; the author uses this daily both to stretch profit targets and to avoid bad trade levels.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Successful vs. failed auction**: a successful auction has exactly one "winner" at the swing extreme (the last trade before reversal); a failed auction has multiple participants still active at the extreme, meaning the auction never properly concluded.
  - When to use: whenever a swing high/low forms, to judge whether it's "finished" (successful, stable) or "unfinished" (failed, likely to be retested and breached).
  - How: a failed auction shows several participants trading right at the top/bottom of a swing rather than one decisive last trade; the market treats this as an imperfection to be resolved later by pushing price back through that level to create a new high/low.
- **Failed auction as a magnet**: because a failed auction is an unresolved imperfection, price is statistically drawn back toward it and tends to push through it to finally produce a single "winner," converting it into a successful auction.
  - When to use (Profit Target application): if a nearby failed auction sits just beyond your normal Profit Target, extend the target past it, since price is likely to test through the failed auction anyway.
  - When to use (trade filter application): before entering, check for a failed auction against your position — a failed auction below a long level, or above a short level, will tend to pull price toward it and produce a losing trade; avoid such setups.
  - Timeframes: author looks for failed auctions on 30-minute charts for intraday trades and daily charts for swing trades.

## Key Concepts
- **Auction analogy**: price movement compared to a live item auction — bidding continues until only one buyer remains at the highest price; failed auctions are the trading equivalent of an auction ending with multiple bidders still active.
- **Imperfection**: the author's term for an unresolved failed auction — something the market "sorts out" later by revisiting and breaching that level.
- **Magnet effect**: the tendency of price to be drawn toward and through failed auctions to resolve the imperfection.

## Mental Models
- Think of every swing extreme as an auction that either closed cleanly (successful) or left bidders unsatisfied (failed) — failed ones are unfinished business the market will return to.
- Use failed auctions as a directional lean, not just a level: expect price to move *toward and through* them, not simply approach and stop.

## Anti-patterns
- **Ignoring a failed auction sitting against your position**: a failed auction below a long entry (or above a short entry) will tend to pull price through it, producing a loss — the author screens for this before entering.
- **Leaving a Profit Target short of a nearby failed auction**: if a failed auction sits just past your normal target, you may be leaving free pips on the table since price is likely to test through it anyway.

## Worked Example
Intraday trade, long position: the author's standard intraday Profit Target is 10 pips, but a significant failed auction sat just past that target. Reasoning that price would likely test above the failed auction to resolve the imperfection, he moved his Profit Target to one pip above the failed auction level instead of taking the standard target — turning a +10 pip result into a +13 pip result. He notes the same logic scales to swing trading, where the extra distance captured from a failed auction can be roughly +30 pips instead of a few pips.

## Key Takeaways
1. Classify swing extremes as successful (one clean winner) or failed (multiple bidders left unresolved) auctions.
2. Expect price to revisit and push through failed auctions to resolve the imperfection — treat them as directional magnets.
3. Extend Profit Targets past a nearby failed auction rather than leaving pips on the table short of it.
4. Before entering, check for a failed auction against your position (below a long, above a short) and avoid the trade if one is present.
5. Look for failed auctions on 30-minute charts (intraday) and daily charts (swing trading).
