# Chapter 16: Macroeconomic News

## Core Idea
After years of failing to build a profitable strategy that trades macro news directly (fundamentally or technically), the author's approach flipped entirely: don't try to predict or trade the news — instead, systematically avoid exposure around it, then use his existing volume-based levels for reversal trades once the post-news trend clarifies.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **News-avoidance over news-prediction**: the author explicitly gave up trying to profit from predicting news outcomes or market reactions (calling it "close to impossible" to trade profitably long-term) and instead built a process for stepping aside before news and re-engaging methodically after.
- **Three-tier red-news severity system** (author's own classification, beyond the standard yellow/orange/red importance ranking most calendars use):
  1. **Weak red news** (e.g., CB Consumer Confidence, Crude Oil Inventories, Unemployment Claims, Core Retail Sales, Building Permits): safe to hold trades through if feeling aggressive; re-enter 1-5 minutes after release if you closed out.
  2. **Standard red news** (e.g., CPI, GDP, NFP, Unemployment Rate, major-economy presidential speeches on economic topics): never hold intraday trades through this; cancel trades against any resulting one-sided trend and consider reversal trades in the trend's direction instead.
  3. **Monster red news** (e.g., Rate Decision/press conference, FOMC Meeting/Monetary Policy Statement, NFP when it could move a rate decision): capable of starting daily/weekly/monthly trends — avoid trading for hours beforehand and don't resume until the next session (or the session after, for conservative traders); never try to catch the high/low directly, only take reversal trades once the first volatility spike has passed.
- **Reversal-trade adaptation to news** (Tip #10): when a strong news reaction shoots price through your S/R level, don't fight it — wait for price to test that level from the other side, then enter in the new trend's direction (opposite your original bias). Requires fast bias-switching (echoes Ch13's reversal-trade discipline).

## Key Concepts
- **Correlated instrument/news pairs**: certain news moves currencies indirectly, not just directly —
  - Oil news → CAD (Canada is an oil exporter)
  - "World danger" news → JPY strengthens as a safe haven, even when Japan itself is affected (e.g., historical tsunami example)
  - Dairy auction news → NZD (New Zealand's economy is dairy-export dependent)
  - Industrial metals news (steel, copper) → CAD and AUD (both metal exporters)
  - China economic news → AUD (China is Australia's largest trade partner)
- **Unexpected news**: unscheduled events (central-bank speeches, geopolitical shocks, natural disasters) that can't be calendared — handled by monitoring a news feed for a volatility spike's cause and standing aside if one appears until things calm down.
- **Widened spreads / slippage during news**: cited as the practical, unavoidable reason even a good strategy underperforms if traded through news — wide spreads (e.g., 0.5 → 5 pips) and slipped stops "literally kill" a profitable day-trading edge.

## Mental Models
- Treat news outcome and market reaction as fundamentally unpredictable — spend zero effort trying to forecast either; spend all effort managing exposure around the event instead.
- Use the historical-volatility-per-news-type approach (an "economic news impact" reference/tool) to pre-plan whether to hold through a scheduled release or exit ahead of it.
- After a strong news spike, think in terms of "let the market pick a side, then trade the retest of my level from the new side" rather than trying to guess the outcome in advance.

## Anti-patterns
- **Trying to fundamentally or technically predict news outcomes**: explicitly tried and abandoned by the author after extensive effort on both approaches.
- **Holding intraday trades through Standard or Monster red news**: wide spreads, slippage, and the chance of a strong one-sided move blowing through any level make this a losing proposition regardless of level quality.
- **Re-entering a post-news trade on a weak, indecisive reaction**: the author only re-enters after seeing a sharp, fast rejection confirming direction — not a slow rotation.
- **Trying to catch the exact high/low during a Monster news spike**: too unpredictable while banks/algorithms are all trading aggressively in the first moments; wait for the spike to pass.

## Key Takeaways
1. Classify every red-news event into Weak / Standard / Monster before deciding whether to hold, close, or avoid trading around it.
2. Exit intraday positions 2-5 minutes before a significant release, using the nearest 1-minute-chart channel edge or resistance/rotation as your exit point.
3. Check historical news-driven volatility ahead of time to size your reaction plan, not to predict direction.
4. Watch for indirect currency exposure (oil→CAD, danger→JPY, dairy→NZD, metals→CAD/AUD, China→AUD) even when the news doesn't name your pair directly.
5. If a level gets shot through by a strong news-driven move, flip to a reversal trade on the retest rather than fighting the new trend.
6. Only re-enter a pre-news position if the post-news reaction is sharp and decisive, not a weak/slow rotation.
