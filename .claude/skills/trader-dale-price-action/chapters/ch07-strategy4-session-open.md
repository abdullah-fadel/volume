# Chapter 7: Strategy 4 — Session Open

## Core Idea
The opening point of each of the three trading sessions (Asian, European, US) acts as a support/resistance orientation point and mood indicator for that session — used only as a confirming signal, never standalone.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Session Open S/R**: the price level at which a session opens becomes support or resistance depending on which way price initially moves.
  - When to use: as an additional confirmation layer on top of the author's primary volume-based strategies.
  - How:
    1. Mark the opening price of the Asian (Tokyo open), European (London open), and US (New York open) sessions on the chart.
    2. If price moves down after the session opens, that open level becomes resistance.
    3. If price moves up after the session opens, that open level becomes support.
    4. Watch for reactions when price retraces back to that session-open level.

## Key Concepts
- **Asian session**: begins at the Tokyo exchange open.
- **European session**: begins at the London exchange open.
- **US session**: begins at the New York exchange open.
- **Session mood**: the initial directional move after a session open gives a read on that session's prevailing bias.

## Mental Models
- Use session-open levels as orientation points from which to read a session's developing mood, not as trade triggers on their own.
- "More confirmations, the better" — stack session-open S/R with the author's main volume-based level for higher-conviction entries.

## Anti-patterns
- **Trading Session Open as a standalone strategy**: the author explicitly states he doesn't trade this in isolation — it's a confirmation layer only.

## Key Takeaways
1. Mark all three session opens (Tokyo, London, New York) as potential S/R orientation points.
2. Direction of the first move after a session open determines whether that level acts as support or resistance.
3. Use exclusively as a confirming signal alongside stronger (volume-based) setups.
