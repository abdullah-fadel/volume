# Chapter 5: Strategy 2 — Open-Drive

## Core Idea
An open-drive is a sudden, strong, one-sided price move (often following sideways accumulation or a session open) whose *starting point* becomes a strong support/resistance level worth returning to after the market confirms the new price range as fair value.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Open-drive**: a sharp, steady, one-directional price move, most commonly appearing right after a sideways price action range or at the start of a trading session (Asian/EU/US).
  - When to use: after spotting a sideways range that suddenly resolves into a strong directional candle/move, or at a session's open when price moves one-sided immediately.
  - How:
    1. Identify the candle/point where the aggressive one-sided move started — this is the open-drive point.
    2. If buyers initiated it (price shoots up), the open point is strong support; if sellers initiated it (price shoots down), the open point is strong resistance.
    3. Wait for a few candles to form beyond the open-drive to confirm the market accepted the new price level as temporary fair value.
    4. Wait for price to return to the open-drive start point and enter there (long if it was support-forming, short if resistance-forming).
  - Why it works: the participants who drove the initial aggressive move will defend their entry point if price returns to it, since they're already positioned and profitable from that level.

## Key Concepts
- **One-sided movement**: the defining trait of an open-drive — price driven steadily and aggressively in a single direction, not a choppy or two-way move.
- **Session open**: Asian, EU, or US session start — a common trigger point for open-drive moves, giving a read on that session's likely directional bias.
- **Fair value acceptance**: the few candles needed after the drive to confirm the market isn't immediately rejecting the new price level.
- **Macro-triggered open-drive**: sometimes a news event causes the open-drive; the author treats it identically regardless of cause — still a valid support/resistance area.

## Mental Models
- Think of the open-drive start point as the origin of the aggressive players' position — a place they're likely to defend again if retested.
- Use a "low volatility area" (similar to sideways action but tighter) preceding a strong move the same way as a full sideways range — it's still an accumulation signature.

## Anti-patterns
- **Entering on the drive candle itself**: the setup is to wait for the pullback to the open-drive origin, not to chase the initial move.
- **Ignoring the cause**: don't discount an open-drive as "just news noise" — the author treats macro-triggered and organic open-drives identically as valid S/R.

## Worked Example
EUR/USD 30-minute chart: 4–5 hours of sideways price action is followed by a large one-sided red (selling) candle — the open-drive. After waiting for several candles to confirm the market accepted the lower prices, price eventually returns to the level where that big red candle opened; that level acts as resistance, and the trade is a short entered on the retest. A second example: EUR/USD opens after the weekend with a one-sided selling open-drive; after the initial gap closes, price returns to test the open-drive start point, which reacts as resistance, confirming the level.

## Key Takeaways
1. Mark the exact point where the aggressive one-sided move began — that is the tradeable level, not the move itself.
2. Wait for a handful of candles beyond the drive to confirm fair-value acceptance before trusting the level.
3. Enter only on the pullback retest of the open-drive origin, in the direction that continues the original aggression.
4. Session opens are a natural place to watch for open-drive formation.
