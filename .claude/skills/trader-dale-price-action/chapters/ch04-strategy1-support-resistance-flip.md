# Chapter 4: Strategy 1 — Support Becoming Resistance (and Vice Versa)

## Core Idea
A support or resistance level that gets strongly breached doesn't lose significance — it flips role (support becomes resistance, or resistance becomes support) and is worth trading on the pullback retest.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Support-becoming-resistance flip**: a level defended two or more times, then breached with strong effort, becomes the opposite type of level and should be defended again on retest.
  - When to use: whenever price forms a level of two-plus strong reactions and later trades through it.
  - How (author's stated steps):
    1. Identify a level where price strongly reacted/jumped away one or more times (two-plus reactions preferred for confidence).
    2. Wait for price to breach that level.
    3. Treat the breached level as still significant — breaching a strong level required real effort/volume from strong buyers or sellers, so it will likely be "defended" again, just from the opposite side.
    4. Wait for price to pull back to this flipped level and enter there (short from newly-flipped resistance; long from newly-flipped support).
    5. Works on all timeframes; author favors daily and 30-minute charts for this setup.
  - Why it works: reversing role requires real institutional effort to force the breach — that same commitment tends to defend the level again from the new side.

## Key Concepts
- **Strong reaction**: a level where price visibly and sharply reversed, indicating real support/resistance rather than a random pause.
- **Flip level**: the same price zone, now defended from the opposite side after a breach.
- **Price Action strategies as confirmation**: the author states he uses all pure-Price-Action strategies (this one included) primarily as *confirmation* for his main Volume-Profile-based strategies, not as the primary signal — though he notes this one works well standalone too.

## Mental Models
- Think of a strongly-defended level as a "line in the sand" institutions committed capital to defend; once broken, the same commitment logic flips to the other side.
- Use multiple reactions (2+) at a level as a confidence multiplier before trusting the flip.

## Anti-patterns
- **Trading the breach itself**: the strategy is about waiting for the *retest* of the flipped level, not chasing the breakout candle.
- **Treating a single weak touch as a valid level**: one small reaction isn't enough — look for a level defended more than once for higher confidence.

## Worked Example
EUR/USD, 30-minute chart: price makes two strong rejections at a level, confirming strong support. Price later breaks through it, and the level becomes resistance — entry is a short on the pullback retest of this new resistance. Hours later, on the same chart, that same level is retested from below four times, confirming it is now defended as resistance; when price eventually pushes back through it again, it re-establishes as support — entry becomes a long on the pullback to this re-flipped support level. The core mechanic in both directions: strong prior defense + confirmed breach + wait for retest, don't chase the breakout candle itself.

## Key Takeaways
1. A breached strong support/resistance flips role rather than disappearing — trade the retest, not the break.
2. Require 2+ reactions at a level before trusting it as significant.
3. Works across all timeframes; the author's preferred timeframes for this setup are daily and 30-minute.
4. Use as confirmation alongside Volume-Profile-based setups for higher-conviction entries.
