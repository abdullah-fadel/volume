# Chapter 13: Volume Profile Trading Setups

## Core Idea
The author's three core, "never enter a trade without one of these" setups — Volume Accumulation, Trend, and Rejection — all locate the exact price within a larger zone where volume was heaviest (via Flexible Volume Profile), then trade the retest of that precise level with no additional confirmation required; when a level fails outright, the same support-becomes-resistance logic converts the loss into a reversal trade.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Volume Setup #1 — Volume Accumulation Setup**: locate a sideways rotation area followed by a strong directional move (buyers accumulating → uptrend, sellers accumulating → downtrend); apply Flexible Volume Profile *inside* the rotation area to find the single price with the heaviest volume; mark that exact level and enter (long if buyers accumulated, short if sellers accumulated) on first touch, no confirmation needed.
- **Volume Setup #2 — Trend Setup**: within a strong trend (naturally a thin profile), find the volume cluster(s) where price paused briefly and participants added to their positions, provided the trend continued afterward; mark the heaviest-volume price within that cluster; enter in the trend direction on first touch, no confirmation needed.
  - Bonus confluence: combining Trend Setup with Strategy 1 (support becoming resistance) creates the author's most powerful S/R zones — worth extra weight when both align.
- **Volume Setup #3 — Rejection Setup**: find a strong, aggressive price rejection (any candle shape, timeframe-independent); apply Flexible Volume Profile to the rejection itself to find where volume was heaviest within that reversal; mark that level and enter on the retest (long after a rejection of lower prices, short after a rejection of higher prices). Author's own note: this is the hardest of the three setups to master, since judging rejection strength and reading volume distribution within it (especially with multiple strong volume areas) takes practice.
- **Reversal trades**: when price fails to react to a volume-based level at all and runs straight through to a Stop-loss, wait for price to return to that same original level and enter the *opposite* direction (original long → reversal short, and vice versa) — the same support-becomes-resistance flip logic (Ch4) applied to failed volume setups.
  - Validity condition: the level must have been shot through cleanly (must not have produced even a small 1-2 pip reaction before failing) — if it reacted at all first, don't take the reversal.

## Key Concepts
- **Why volume levels get defended (two reasons cited for all three setups)**:
  1. Participants who built a position at a level are motivated to defend it — driving price back in their original direction when retested.
  2. Opposing participants caught on the wrong side near a strong volume level don't want to "fight" aggressive players there, so they exit — and an exit from a short position means buying (pushing price up), while an exit from a long position means selling (pushing price down) — reinforcing the same directional push.
- **First touch, no confirmation**: distinctive to all three core setups — once the heaviest-volume level is marked, the author enters on the very first retest, not after additional confirming candles (contrast with Ch9's daily/weekly high-low strategy, which does require 1-3 confirming candles after a breach).
- **Bias-changing discipline**: the psychological difficulty of reversal trades — traders tend to stay attached to their original directional bias even after a Stop-loss; the author's fix is starting reversal trades with a smaller position size to build confidence.

## Mental Models
- Treat the wider structure (rotation area, trend, rejection) as the *search zone*, and Flexible Volume Profile as the tool that zooms in to find the one precise level worth trading within it.
- Use setup confluence (e.g., Trend Setup + Support-becomes-resistance) as a signal to increase conviction, not just take either signal alone.
- When a trade fails cleanly (no reaction at all), think of the failure itself as new information: the level's defenders were overwhelmed, so the trade should flip, not be abandoned.

## Anti-patterns
- **Requiring extra confirmation on Setup #1/#2/#3 entries**: the author explicitly enters at first touch — waiting for confirmation isn't part of these three setups.
- **Taking a reversal trade after any partial reaction**: if price reacted even 1-2 pips before failing, the level "worked, only a bit sooner" — not a reversal candidate.
- **Staying attached to the original bias after a Stop-loss**: failing to flip bias quickly is called out as the primary psychological obstacle to trading reversals well.

## Worked Example
Confluence trade (Trend Setup + Support-becomes-resistance), EUR/USD monthly chart: a level had three strong rejections (marking it as strong support); when price later broke through it, it flipped to resistance per Ch4's framework. Independently, a volume cluster from Setup #2 sat within the same strong trend leading up to that level. Seeing both signals align at the same zone, the author entered a short at the peak of the volume cluster. Four months later, price returned to and reacted at that resistance zone as expected, and the position was +851 pips in profit — illustrating that the same setups scale from intraday (30-minute) to swing (240-minute/daily) to long-term investment (monthly) timeframes without changing the underlying logic.

## Key Takeaways
1. Never enter without one of the three core setups (Accumulation, Trend, Rejection) present — this is the author's explicit trading rule.
2. Use Flexible Volume Profile to zoom into the exact heaviest-volume price within the qualifying structure — that precise level, not the whole zone, is the entry trigger.
3. Enter on first touch for all three core setups — no extra confirmation required.
4. Watch for confluence between a volume setup and a Price Action setup (e.g., support-becomes-resistance) — these are the highest-conviction trades.
5. When a level fails outright (price shoots through with no reaction), flip to a reversal trade at the same level rather than treating it as a dead level.
6. Size reversal trades smaller at first to make the bias-switch psychologically easier.
