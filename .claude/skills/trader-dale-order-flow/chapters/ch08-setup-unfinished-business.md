# Chapter 8: Trading Setup — Unfinished Business

## Core Idea
Unfinished Business is deliberately not a fifth standalone entry strategy — it's a magnet-like market imperfection the author uses to sharpen Take Profit, Stop Loss, trend confirmation, and bad-trade avoidance decisions inside the other four setups.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Unfinished Business as a Decision Aid (four applications)**: use the "magnet" behavior of an improperly formed high/low to inform trade management, not entries.
  - When to use: whenever you already have a trade thesis from one of the standalone setups (Ch 6, Ch 7) and want to refine timing or risk around it.
  - How — four applications:
    1. **Take Profit**: if you're in profit and price is heading toward a nearby Unfinished Business marker, consider holding a little longer since price is likely to test it — works best when the marker is already close, since there's no guarantee a distant one gets tested.
    2. **Stop Loss**: avoid placing your SL just in front of an Unfinished Business marker — price is prone to punch through the SL en route to "fixing" the marker behind it.
    3. **Confirmation**: if price pulls back against your position but an Unfinished Business marker sits beyond the pullback in your original direction, treat the move as a likely temporary pullback rather than a trend change, since price is likely to continue toward the marker.
    4. **Warning against a bad trade**: avoid entering Long when an Unfinished Business marker sits below your entry, or Short when one sits above — the magnet effect risks price shooting past your entry to test it before continuing your way (or against you first).

## Key Concepts
- **Unfinished Business / Failed Auction**: a high or low that formed without the opposite side reaching zero contracts traded (recap from Ch 4) — the market's way of leaving an "imperfection" it tends to revisit.
- **Magnet behavior**: the core property exploited by all four applications — price has an elevated tendency to travel toward and through an unresolved Unfinished Business marker.

## Mental Models
- Think of Unfinished Business as background context you check before finalizing a TP, SL, or entry decision from another setup — not a chart pattern you scan for and trade on its own.
- **Why it works**: an improperly formed high/low means the auction process (Ch 4) never resolved cleanly at that price — the "unfinished" imbalance creates a standing incentive for price to return and complete it.
- **Failure mode**: treating Unfinished Business as a Holy Grail entry signal. The author is explicit that price can move a long distance without ever testing a marker, so using it as a primary trigger — rather than a supporting read — will produce false expectations.

## Anti-patterns
- **Placing a Stop Loss just short of an Unfinished Business marker**: price is disproportionately likely to run through that exact zone to fix the imperfection, taking the SL out first.
- **Entering counter to a nearby Unfinished Business marker** (e.g., Long with one sitting below): risks the magnet pulling price down to test it before (or instead of) continuing upward.
- **Treating Unfinished Business as a standalone strategy**: the author explicitly recommends mastering the other four trading setups first and using this only as a supporting layer.

## Worked Example
**Extending a profitable Short toward an Unfinished Business marker (live trading example, EUR Futures):** The author was in a Short trade and weighing whether to take a straight 10-pip profit or split the exit (bank 10, let the rest run to 20). An Unfinished Business marker sat at a prior turning point above current price. Reasoning that the marker would likely act as a magnet and could pull price back up against the Short, he closed the full position at the 10-pip target rather than risk the runner. Price subsequently moved up and tested that exact Unfinished Business marker, confirming that holding for the extra 10 pips would have cost back the gain — validating the early-exit decision.

## Key Takeaways
1. Unfinished Business marks an improperly formed high/low and tends to act like a magnet, pulling price back to "fix" it.
2. Use it for four supporting decisions: stretching a Take Profit, avoiding a bad Stop Loss placement, confirming a trend continuation, and screening out risky entries — never as a standalone entry trigger.
3. The magnet effect works best when the marker is already close to current price; distant markers carry no guarantee of being tested.
4. Master the four standalone trading setups (Ch 6–7) before leaning on Unfinished Business — it's explicitly a bonus layer, not a fifth core strategy.
