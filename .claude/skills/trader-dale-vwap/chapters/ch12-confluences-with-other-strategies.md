# Chapter 12: Confluences with Other Strategies

## Core Idea
Standalone VWAP setups are battle-tested but inconsistent on their own; combining them with the author's Price Action (support-becomes-resistance) and Volume Profile (Trend setup) frameworks — or even combining multiple VWAP anchors with each other — produces "confluence" zones where two or more independent methods agree on the same level, meaningfully raising the odds of a clean reaction.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Confluence**: when two or more independent trading setups (VWAP, Price Action, Volume Profile) converge on the same general Support/Resistance area — not necessarily the exact same price, but the same general zone.
  - When to use: as the author's own default filter for higher-consistency entries — he states standalone VWAP setups can win often but leave you questioning results during dry spells; combining them with unrelated setups "delivered the best results for me."
  - How: identify a VWAP-based level (any anchor type from Ch3–Ch9, or a 1st-deviation level from Ch10–Ch11) → separately identify a Price Action or Volume Profile level using those frameworks' own criteria → check whether the two zones overlap or sit close together → treat the overlap as a higher-conviction trade zone than either signal alone.
- **Price Action setup: Support becomes Resistance (and vice versa)**: a level that produced one or more strong reactions in the past, then got breached, flips role and is defended again from the new side.
  - When to use: as a VWAP confluence partner, or standalone; the author finds it "nearly every day" on charts. Works across timeframes; his preference is daily and 30-minute charts.
  - How: spot a strong price reaction/reversal at a level (two-plus reactions add confidence) → wait for the level to be breached → treat the breached level as still significant, now defended from the opposite side → enter on the retest, in the direction consistent with the flip.
- **Volume Profile Trend setup**: locate a volume cluster that forms *within* an established trend (not in a rotation), and trade the pullback to it in the trend's direction.
  - When to use: specifically pairs well with VWAP because both tend to perform best in trending markets (the author notes the VWAP Rotation strategy is the one VWAP exception to this trend-preference). Detailed fully in the author's Volume Profile book; summarized here for confluence purposes.
  - How: confirm the market is in a strong trend (one side clearly more aggressive) → look for a volume cluster — a pause within the trend where volume built up as participants added to positions → mark that cluster as Support (uptrend) or Resistance (downtrend) → enter at the start of the cluster on a retest, in the trend direction.
- **Combos of multiple trading setups**: layering three or more of the author's own frameworks (Anchored VWAP variants, 1st-deviation strategies, Price Action, Volume Profile) onto the same chart and trading only where several agree.
  - When to use: whenever you want the highest-conviction version of a VWAP-based level — including combining two different VWAP anchors with each other (e.g., a Yearly VWAP converging with a swing-point-anchored VWAP).
  - How: plot multiple independent setups on the same chart → look for zones (not exact points) where they converge → prioritize entries at convergence zones over single-signal levels.

## Key Concepts
- **Independent setups**: the confluence logic only strengthens a level if the contributing setups are genuinely independent methods (VWAP vs. Price Action vs. Volume Profile) rather than variations of the same read.
- **Zone, not point**: confluence doesn't require setups to point at the exact same price — "converge within the same general area" is sufficient.
- **Multi-VWAP confluence**: two differently-anchored VWAP lines (e.g., Yearly VWAP + swing-point-anchored VWAP) moving toward each other and forming a combined zone is itself a valid confluence, without needing Price Action or Volume Profile at all.

## Mental Models
- Treat a single VWAP touch as a candidate, not a trigger — the author's own practice is to actively search for a second, independent confirming setup before treating a level as tradeable.
- When multiple anchored VWAPs converge, think of it as the "average trader" reference from two different starting points agreeing — a stronger form of the same underlying VWAP logic rather than a separate confluence type.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating any two overlapping lines as confluence**: the setups must be genuinely independent (different underlying logic) — stacking two VWAPs anchored almost identically isn't meaningfully different from one.
- **Forcing confluence where none exists**: not every VWAP touch will have a Price Action or Volume Profile setup nearby — the author's examples show plenty of valid VWAP-only trades; confluence raises conviction, it isn't a requirement for every entry (see Ch13 for how this plays into entry confirmation).
- **Ignoring which regime favors which setup**: Volume Profile Trend setup and most VWAP strategies favor trending markets — pairing them in a rotation regime undercuts both.

## Worked Example
GBP/USD, Daily chart with Yearly VWAP: price trades in a narrow range early in the year, then drops sharply, launching a new downtrend and validating the VWAP Trend strategy (Ch11). A first short off the 1st deviation works using VWAP alone — no confluence needed. But the second test is where confluence appears: price retraces further to a zone where the Price Action support-becomes-resistance setup also applies (a previously broken support level, now acting as resistance) exactly overlapping the VWAP-based resistance zone. With both an independent Price Action signal and the VWAP deviation level agreeing on the same resistance zone, this second short is judged the higher-conviction trade of the two — illustrating the core distinction the author draws between a valid standalone VWAP signal and a stronger confluence-backed one.

## Key Takeaways
1. Confluence = two or more independent setups (VWAP, Price Action, Volume Profile, or multiple VWAP anchors) agreeing on the same general zone — the author's preferred way to raise consistency beyond standalone VWAP signals.
2. Price Action support-becomes-resistance: strong reaction(s) at a level → breach → level flips role → trade the retest.
3. Volume Profile Trend setup: volume cluster within an established trend → trade the pullback to the cluster in the trend direction.
4. VWAP performs best in trending markets (with the Rotation strategy as the explicit exception) — the same trend-preference applies to the Volume Profile Trend setup, making them a natural pairing.
5. Confluence zones don't require exact price overlap — general-area convergence is sufficient.
6. Multiple differently-anchored VWAP lines converging is itself a valid confluence, independent of Price Action or Volume Profile.
